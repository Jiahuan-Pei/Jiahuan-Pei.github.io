Users value mental-health chatbots for their accessibility, affordability and non-judgmental nature, yet trust in them should be earned and calibrated, not maximised. Building on a multi-stakeholder framework of trust criteria spanning human, interaction and AI dimensions, this talk presents glass-box multi-agent systems that make AI reasoning inspectable and steerable: explainable DSM-5 diagnosis, motivational-interviewing dialogue, double-empathy communication practice and conversational autism screening. It then turns to ongoing work on how linguistic synchrony shapes human–AI bonding and how gaze signals can reveal what users leave unsaid. We conclude with open challenges and an invitation to interdisciplinary collaboration.
